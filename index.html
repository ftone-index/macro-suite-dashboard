1. Scale: The F‑Tone Index ranges from 0 (extremely dovish) to 100 (extremely hawkish). It measures the Federal Reserve’s policy tone as expressed in official speeches, FOMC statements, and minutes, plus regional Fed speeches, testimony, and Fed blog posts.

2. Dual display: The headline shows live → 7d avg — the live score is the immediate average of today's document scores; the 7d avg is a 7‑day moving average.

3. Qualitative label: The “Hawkish” / “Dovish” label shows in which segment of the 0–100 range the 7d avg is anchored. It is a quick visual and mnemonic reference for the score's segment. It does not imply a movement or a trend direction.

4. Persistent alert: If the live score moves 5 points or more in the opposite direction of the smoothed score's movement (i.e., the 7d avg trend), a persistent alert (⚠️) appears next to the live score for up to 24 hours — as an early warning of a potential trend change.

5. Market Expectation: A companion score derived from Treasury yields and the US Dollar Index, showing what markets are pricing.

6. Fed policy rates: Shows the Fed Funds target range, IORB rate, and ON RRP rate for quick reference.

7. Confidence: HIGH, MEDIUM, or LOW based on the breadth of sources analysed and the total text volume.

8. Radical transparency: Every score is traceable to its source document. No black boxes.

9. Data availability: When a fresh index computation cannot be retrieved for any reason, the dashboard displays the most recent successfully computed reading, marked as ‘last reading’.

10. Inclusive design: The colour pair used for the live and smoothed scores is carefully chosen to be clearly distinguishable for users with the most common form of colour vision deficiency (red‑green).
